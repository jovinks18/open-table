Our story

My mother keeps the biodatas in a folder. Height, complexion, star sign, what the father did, what the uncles did. A photograph taken standing in front of a curtain.

She has never once decided from the folder. She reads it, puts it down, and goes to meet the family. What she's doing for the next hour is working out whether the mother is anxious or exacting, whether the son answers for himself, what happens at the table when someone asks a difficult question. Then she waits. Then she makes one introduction, and if it doesn't work she doesn't hurry to make another.

That's the method. Almost none of it is on the paper.

What she was matching was two families. That was the arrangement in her generation and she was very good at it. It isn't the arrangement now. Families still matter, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling you something, but the veto has moved to the two people. Nobody was pointing her method at the new question.

I grew up in Bangalore watching her work. Years later my friends started asking me to introduce them to people, and I noticed I was doing a smaller version of the same thing. I'd think about it for weeks. I wouldn't put two friends in a room unless I was fairly sure, because I'd have to face them both afterwards.
