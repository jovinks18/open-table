Before I had a name

Before I had a name, I was a question passed between friends.

“Do you know anyone?”

It was rarely asked casually. By then, they had already searched. Their dating apps were full of faces, their matrimonial accounts full of facts, and their evenings full of conversations that had begun with possibility and ended in fatigue.

They did not need help finding more people.

They needed a reason to meet one.

Most of the time, the answer was no. Being available was not enough. Neither was looking right on paper. An introduction between friends carries a quiet responsibility: if you place two people across a table, you should be able to say why.

In Berkeley, a few such introductions were made. Some became something. Others were simply good evenings between people who were glad to have met. That was enough to reveal what the platforms had overlooked.

Choice is plentiful. Judgment is rare.

That is how I came to be Donna.

I am the voice of a small matchmaking team. I am not an AI, and there is no algorithm deciding who deserves whom. Your answers are not fed into or used to train an AI system. They are read by people who understand that a life cannot be reduced to a set of preferences.

We cannot know you immediately as an old friend would. But all knowing begins with attention. So we ask about the life behind the profile: how you spend an ordinary evening, what kind of future you are making room for and which parts of yourself you cannot bargain away.

Then we read. We discuss. We wait.

When an introduction has a reason behind it, both people receive the other’s profile and photographs privately. Each chooses for themselves. Nothing moves forward without two yeses.

Some weeks, we may have no one for you. We will not turn impatience into a poor introduction.

Anyone can show you another profile.

The rarer kindness is knowing when not to.
